# Documents: PDF, office, e-books, video

The HTTP tier downloads the bytes; the converter depends on what they are. Detection is by magic
bytes first (`%PDF`, `PK`, `{\rtf`), then `Content-Type`, then the URL extension — never by trust in
the extension alone. An archive says what it is in its own `mimetype` file: `application/epub+zip`
is an EPUB, `...opendocument.text` an ODT (an archive used to be an EPUB whatever its `mimetype`, and an ODT came
out `empty`).

A file on this machine goes through the same converters: the CLI takes a path or
a `file://` URL, and nothing goes to the network - no reader, archive, browser or picture download.
The file is copied, never moved. Over MCP only a file inside read-page's own folders is read (a
page folder of its cache, or a folder with its manifest), checked after links are resolved.

A file whose name or type says ZIP and whose bytes do not is named: a Word, Excel or PowerPoint 97-2003 file, a password-protected Office file (an encrypted package is an OLE2 file), gzip, 7-Zip, RAR, a picture, a program, text (its text is the answer), or data of no known type | `signals.broken_archive` (`how`: cut, damaged, not a ZIP; the members found, whole, and the ones that are not, with why; `all_members` when every member was looked at - a file cut or damaged in its table of contents, its members all there); a cut or damaged archive whose whole members hold under 40 words of their own (the markers of its pictures and their list are not its words) is `error`, more is `ok`, and the advice says how much was read - or that the whole members gave no text - and what gets the rest: a whole copy of a file on this machine, `--refresh` for a server's (the same bytes come back while its copy is cut), nothing when only its table of contents is cut or damaged; no machine's pandoc or markitdown is asked (they read archives as `zipfile` does, and markitdown spent 4 s to say "no converter for this file type"). A member is read up to 32 MB, all of them up to 128 MB, 10 000 members at most (folder entries count), and every byte unpacked to judge them counts, kept or not, up to 256 MB an archive; the read's clock is looked at every member; a member whose size follows its data (a data descriptor) is read to where its deflated data ends, and one too big to read so ends the reading; a size past the end of the file is a cut member, and a header where a deflated member's data ends the next member whatever its own header said. Measured 2026-11-21/22: a DOCX cut in its picture reads its whole text, an XLSX cut in its third sheet its first two; 10 000 members in 0.3 s; a member that unpacks to 256 MB, in 0.2 s and 98 MB; 2 000 members of 32 MB of zeros (62 MB of file) 0.98 s, where they took 58 s |

| images inside a document | `zip_images()` reads `word/media/…`, `…/images/…` into `img/` | deduplicated, `≤2×2` dropped. A Google Doc exported as docx carried 11 pictures this way. A docx is ordered by the flow of `a:blip` references in `document.xml` (resolved through `document.xml.rels`), so the list and the MCP image blocks are in reading order, an EPUB by the spine of its package document (a chapter the spine names again is scanned once); markers inside the text are still not invented. ## Google Docs, Sheets, Slides: the export endpoint is the primary path

A `/document/d/ID/edit` URL returns a 663 KB JavaScript shell (200 OK, no text). The same ID with
`/export?format=docx` returns the document itself (734 KB, 11 pictures in `word/media`), and
`?format=txt` gives plain text without the pictures. So for `docs.google.com` the skill rewrites the
URL before fetching, and only falls back to the page if the export answers with HTML or an error.
A bounce to `accounts.google.com` from **both** means the document is private: that is
`login_required`, not a broken export.

Measured: 11 326 characters, 2.4 s, `chain.tried = ["google:export", "document:docx"]`.

## trafilatura and images

`trafilatura 2.2.0` accepts `include_images=True` and silently ignores it: no `![]()` appears in
its output. The call is kept anyway — the flag is the documented way and future versions may honour
it — but picture markers depend on the extractor that keeps images (`defuddle` does, trafilatura
does not). Pictures are downloaded either way; only their positions in the text are affected.

## OCR: on when tesseract is there

Detecting a missing text layer takes 14 ms and rendering a page 33 ms; both always happen, and the
PNGs land in `files.pages` for a model that can look. With OCR - `tesserocr` in-process, which
`readpage.py --setup` installs with fourteen language packs, four vertical ones and Fraktur's from tessdata_fast, or
the `tesseract` command - the same pages are OCR'd side by side and the result becomes the text (`engine:
document:ocr`, `signals.ocr_pages`), so a text-only pipeline gets words instead of a picture.
Measured on a synthetic scan: "SCANNED REPORT / Total 42 units" read back correctly.

**Languages, page by page.** `--setup` fetches the ten languages with the most web text by Common Crawl's
CC-MAIN-2026-43 - `eng rus chi_sim chi_tra jpn kor deu fra spa por` - and four scripts English reads as nothing -
`ara ell tha heb` -, 27.4 MiB, the vertical packs of Chinese, Japanese and Korean and Fraktur's (`jpn_vert
chi_tra_vert chi_sim_vert kor_vert frk`, 13.7 MiB), the packs of the user's own languages beside them
(`READ_PAGE_LANG`, else the system's list; at most three), and `osd`, tesseract's own script and orientation detection
(10.1 MiB). A scanned page is never read with all of them at once (on 52 pages in 13 languages that took 2.2-16 s a
page, 5.2 on average for the ten languages, against 1.2-3.5 and 2.15 chosen per page, and read Korean at 35 % CER -
English before Korean -, Arabic and Hindi at 81-84 %). OSD names the page's script first (0.25-0.6 s), and the page is
read with that script's pack - `chi_sim`, `chi_tra` and `jpn` alone
(they read the English words in their text themselves, and English beside them made the rest worse), every other one
with English beside it; a Han page is read with `chi_sim`, then with `chi_tra` and `jpn` while the best reading is
unsure of itself (OSD does not tell simplified from traditional, and it is a page's kana that make it Japanese to OSD:
a kanji-heavy one can come out as Han); a
Latin page with English and the user's own Latin-script packs, then again with the pack of the language its words are
in (`intl.latin_pack`: the commonest words of 15 languages) when that pack is here; a page whose script OSD is not sure
of, with English and the user's own packs. A script whose pack is not here - a Hindi scan on a machine of the
fourteen - is read with English and named in `improve` (`readpage.py --setup --ocr-langs eng+hin`).

**Real scans** (2026-11-23). A page scanned on its side or upside down is turned upright by OSD's orientation when OSD
is that sure of it (from 5.0) and its script told again upright; where the page read turned is unsure of itself it is
read as it lay too, and the surer reading kept (OSD took pages of a magazine set in Fraktur for upside down). A page
printed in vertical columns - tesseract's layout finds its text written top to bottom - is read again with the vertical
packs `--setup` fetches (`jpn_vert`, `chi_tra_vert`, `chi_sim_vert`, `kor_vert`), its script's first, the most
confident kept. A page whose script OSD is unsure of, or one it calls Latin whose reading is unsure of itself, is read
again with the likeliest other scripts' packs - OSD's own answer's, then the scripts OSD was seen to take for it (Hebrew
for Arabic, Greek for Latin, Fraktur for Latin, ...), at most three -, the most confident kept. A page OSD names Fraktur
is read with `frk`. The cost falls on such pages alone: an upright page in a script OSD is sure of is read as before.
Measured on real pages (CER): 42 turned copies 67 -> 22 %, 27 vertical pages 104 -> 67, 6 pages OSD was unsure of or
wrong about 90 -> 46, 17 Fraktur pages 32 -> 21.
`READ_PAGE_OCR_LANGS`, when
set, decides every page; a picture (`--ocr N`) is read with the pack of its page's text - its script, or its Latin
language - and English beside it. The measurement is in [engines.md](engines.md#ocr-languages-measured).
`READ_PAGE_TESSDATA` points at a data folder outside the install (the environment sets its own). Without OCR nothing
changes: the answer names the fix, and a vision model can still read `files.pages` more accurately than OCR.

OCR counts as working only once tesseract has started on a pack (`caps()`, the setup report and the
read all ask it, not a listing of the folder). Where it is installed and does not start - on Windows a
profile folder with characters outside ASCII (`Иван`), which tesserocr cannot hand tesseract in the
ANSI code page, goes by its 8.3 short name and, with none, cannot start; a pack cut short - a scan
reads `missing: ocr` with the reason (`signals.ocr_error`), not a bare `empty`, and the fix named for
a path is `READ_PAGE_HOME` on an ASCII folder (on Windows of at most 120 characters), then `--setup` there.

## Exact quotes
