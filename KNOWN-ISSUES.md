# Known issues and weak spots

Honest list, kept next to the code. Every row says what is wrong, what it costs, and what to do
about it. Status: **fixed** (this cut), **planned** (backlog), **by design** (accepted, with the
reason), **deferred** (a feature, agreed to be built later). Row numbers are stable: code and tests
refer to them, so a row that is retired leaves a gap.

State: the 0.4.6 release and the 0.5.0 work after it, 2026-11-22. The numbers come from the test
suites and from the measurements dated in the rows.

| # | Area | Problem | Cost | Treatment | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | extraction | `cover`/`fragment` counted characters, not words: a nav-heavy list showed 0.30, a page with 10 000 short comments showed 0.17 although the article was complete | the agent was told to read `page.md` when it did not need to | fixed: `cover` is `text words / visible words` (visible text = the page with tags and scripts stripped). Measured on the 30-URL corpus: 11 of 30 cover values moved (wikipedia 1.0 -> 0.871, tailwind 0.382 -> 0.448, w3schools 0.756 -> 0.543), **no** fragment flipped and the ok-rate stayed 28/30 | fixed |
| 2 | extraction | the extractor was chosen by text length; longer is not always better | occasionally a differently-shaped but equally complete text | fixed: candidates are scored `words + 2 per heading`, so a structured article wins over a slightly longer bag of leftovers. On the 30-URL corpus and the 12-URL comparison the chosen engine did not change (17 defuddle, 6 server, 2 trafilatura, 2 main, 2 document, 1 video; 12/12 ok, 0 silent losses) - the rule is a safety net, not a rewrite | fixed |
| 3 | extraction | the `main` container fallback is gated by two crude rules (>30 links, >1.5x text) | a page can get forms or a footer widget appended | keep `news.jcbo.jp` in `tests/sites.md` as the control case | by design |
| 4 | classification | word thresholds (`empty` <5, `not_found` <40, `blocked` <40 / <25 + title, `login` <40/80) are tuned on fixtures, not on a corpus | a very chatty wall could be missed | every sweep re-checks them; `tests/fixes.py` and `props.py` hold the four counter-examples; `signals.status_reason` shows the rule | by design |
| 5 | pictures | positions exist only where the extractor kept the images (defuddle yes, trafilatura no); docx/epub have no positions at all | a picture list without order, which matters for the MCP image blocks | fixed: a page gets markers where the extractor kept them, otherwise a `## Pictures` list plus attachment from the manifest; a docx is ordered by `document.xml` (the flow of `a:blip` references), an EPUB by the spine of its package document (`epub_image_order`: chapters in reading order, then every `<img>`/`<image xlink:href>` inside them). Entries carry `order` + `signals.images_ordered`. Measured on a synthetic book whose archive order is reversed: `img/z.png` then `img/a.png`, order 1 and 2 | fixed |
| 6 | pictures | the picture phase is 6 s, 32 in parallel, and takes only the printed text's pictures | a text with hundreds of pictures can still be cut: those markers say `not downloaded: no time` | `--download` takes every picture with no time limit; `--deny-images` removes the cost; a picture another page of the site has is a hard link, no request | by design |
| 7 | cache | two concurrent runs of the same URL write into one directory | a half-written file is possible in theory | measured: 4 parallel runs, one result; manifest is written last, SIGKILL leaves no manifest so the next run refetches | by design |
| 8 | cache | one folder per URL, the flags live in the manifest (`_mode`); `--dir`, `--from`, `--file` and `--json` are not part of it | a cached answer is re-read when the flags that change the text differ; pictures are topped up in place | a run without `--deny-images` after one with it reuses the text (measured: 1 page request for both runs); `--refresh` revalidates the page and every picture separately | fixed |
| 9 | cache | entries are pruned only when older than a day, at most once an hour | up to a day of pictures on disk | `prune_cache()`; lower `CACHE_KEEP_HOURS` if disk matters; the OS also clears the temp folder | by design |
| 10 | network | a certificate the machine does not trust is retried with verification off | a MITM could feed the text | only for public URLs, only after a certificate error, recorded in `signals.tls` | by design |
| 11 | network | readers cost money; `--refresh` re-checks an archived page through a reader every time | one credit per refresh of a dead page (Firecrawl, Exa or Tavily, whichever answers first) | no counter in the manifest: setting a key is taken as consent, and README says what costs what. The skill never calls a reader without a flag | by design |
| 12 | browser | a page is waited for only until its text stops growing; there is no wait for a specific XHR or selector | an SPA that fills the DOM after a slower request needs a second `--alternate` | fixed: one 10-second budget per browser covers load *and* settling (~0.3 s for a page whose text is already stable); a shell that is still empty then gets 5 s more - 1.5 s waiting for in-flight requests, 3.5 s watching the body text and the usual mount nodes (`#app`, `#root`, `#__next`, `#___gatsby`, `#svelte`, `#main`, `main`, `article`, `[role=main]`), ending as soon as the text has grown by 200 characters on two polls. Measured: a page that fills `#app` at 11 s reads `ok` with `signals.browser_waited: "mount:#app"`. A *named* selector or XHR the caller knows about is still not a parameter - the skill has no such knowledge, and `--alternate` covers the rest | fixed |
| 13 | login | markers existed for en/ru/ja/zh/de/es/fr only; JS paywalls (NYT) were invisible in HTML | a sign-in page, a members-only page or a missing page in another language read as `ok`; a paywalled article looked like any article | fixed: `LOGIN_PHRASES`, `LOGIN_HINTS` and `NOTFOUND_PHRASES` in the twenty languages of the wall words (folded like them), and `signals.paywall` for schema.org's `isAccessibleForFree: false`. Measured on real pages, the same URLs read before and after (1 368 pages, 2026-11-22): 187 sign-in probes (Google, Facebook, Instagram, Microsoft and local sites' login pages, in 20 languages) are `login_required` 61 -> 92 (19 of 20 languages gain; Japanese 7 -> 7); 211 addresses that do not exist on local sites: 5 of the 25 read `ok` are `not_found` (Kinopoisk, Tencent, 36Kr, Almasry Alyoum, Terra), and the wording of real 404 pages is recognised on 143 of 186 (88 before); 87 of 285 articles of paywalled publishers carry `signals.paywall` (and 4 of 165 free-news pages, all premium articles); a marker changed no answer of 685 ordinary pages (news, Wikipedia and Wiktionary entries, 89 of them under 80 words). What was left out, and why: row 191 | fixed |
| 14 | documents | a scanned PDF is rendered (first three pages) but not read: no text layer, no OCR | a long scan stays unread | fixed: when `tesseract` is installed, the rendered pages are OCR'd (`document:ocr`, `signals.ocr_pages`/`ocr_langs`, first 3 pages, `READ_PAGE_OCR_LANGS` for languages, `READ_PAGE_TESSDATA` for a data folder outside the install). Without it the answer still names the fix in `improve[]`. Measured: a synthetic scan reads back "SCANNED REPORT / Total 42 units"; the same page in Russian reads back "СКАН-ОТЧЁТ № 42 / Итого: 42 единицы" with `READ_PAGE_OCR_LANGS=rus+eng`. A scan is now recognised on the `pdftotext` path too, so a machine without pymupdf also gets `no text layer` + the `ocr` hint instead of a bare `empty` | fixed |
| 15 | media | video is read only through its **subtitles**; a video without captions gives an empty text, and a video **file** (`.mp4`) is saved as `media_only` and never transcribed; MCP has no video block | a talk without captions is unreadable | timestamps and `transcript.vtt` are done (0.4.3). Local speech recognition was measured (2026-11-22) and left out: where a platform's automatic track exists - nearly every video in English, Russian and most languages - Whisper small is not better (pooled WER 8.9 % automatic against 10.9 % over 43 clips in six languages, a tie on the professionally captioned ones, Russian 9.9 against 16.2 %), and it would add 287-345 MB to `--setup`, 4-5 s of CPU per audio minute and 9-10 minutes of speech a read; what it would close is Chinese on YouTube, Bilibili and RuTube videos with no track. Key-frame OCR: a few dozen slide words a lecture, half of them noise. A vector index inside the CLI was rejected - the caller already does the retrieval | by design |
| 16 | MCP | limits: 5 MB per image, 20 images, 10 MB total | a gallery is cut (reported, never silent) | taken as the minimum across Anthropic/OpenAI | by design |
| 17 | MCP | `structuredContent` is ignored by some hosts; `image` blocks are rendered by most, `audio` almost never | the manifest may be invisible | the header carries the essentials; `read(dir,"manifest.json")` is the escape hatch | by design |
| 18 | speed | median 2.2-3.8 s against ~1 s for server-side fetch services: pictures plus several extractors | slower single reads | `--deny-images` gives ~1-2 s; keep the slow path for accuracy | by design |
| 19 | crash path | a safety-net manifest had `status` and `advice` but no `url`/`dir`, so MCP could not `read` after a crash | one unreadable failure | 0.4.2 fills `url.requested`, `dir` and `signals.status_reason` | fixed |
| 20 | platform | only Windows was exercised by hand; Linux paths, snap/flatpak browsers and WebKit are untested | unknown breakage on those systems | fixed: CI runs the offline suites on **ubuntu (3.9 and 3.12), windows and macOS**, a second job runs Playwright's Chromium on all three (`tests/browser_live.py`: shell, late mount, cookie gate), and since 0.4.5 CI fabricates `/snap/bin/chromium` and the flatpak export so the detector is tested too. What is left is a snap-*confined* build (a snap needs snapd and AppArmor, which a container does not have). That is a packaging quirk, not a UI one: every launch is headless, in a fresh context, with no user profile - the skill never drives the browser you are using and never opens a window, so a desktop session is not something it needs | fixed |
| 21 | tests | live suites depend on the network and on sites that change | flaky reds | `tests/sites.md` registry + the offline suites (see README) are what CI runs | by design |
| 22 | cost | the skill text is paid on every session that loads it: it had grown to about 4 200 tokens (17 K characters; an earlier "~1 200" here was never measured) | a weak model reads a long procedure worse, and every session pays for it | `SKILL.md` is the procedure and the first choice of each step, 5.6 K characters (about 1 400 tokens, characters / 4); the answer's files, statuses, flags, privacy, limits, cookies, pictures and video live in [references/answer.md](references/answer.md), nothing dropped | by design |
| 23 | single image endpoint | `media_only` returns the file and the MCP image block, plus a one-line `content.md` naming the file; no caption from `alt`/EXIF | a page that *is* a picture still has no description | the one line stops "read content.md" from failing; captioning is backlog | by design |
| 24 | honesty | `advice` is one sentence chosen by the status branch order; two problems can compete | occasionally the second problem is not mentioned | order: archive -> wall/login/empty -> fragment -> weak login; a stale-cookie hint is appended when cookies were sent | by design |
| 25 | limits | `MAX_PAGE_BYTES` 64 MB of HTML/text parsed in memory; a bigger document is downloaded whole and left as `raw.*` with `too_large` | a pathological page costs ~20 s | the *download* is never capped by `--download`; only turning 100 MB into one Python string is | by design |
| 26 | MCP | `read(dir, file)` used to read any path the model named | a prompt-injected model could read local files | fixed: only folders under `.../read-page/pages` or folders with our `manifest.json` (`generator: read-page`) are readable | fixed |
| 28 | robots.txt | never consulted; rejected on purpose for single reads | a bulk `--download` run ignores a site's rules | maintainer decision: behave like a browser for a single user-requested read, do not add the check; revisit only if a site complains | by design |
| 29 | build | `defuddle`/`markitdown` were called through `shell=True` with quoted paths | a POSIX path containing a quote would break the command | fixed: argv everywhere; `shell=True` only for `.cmd`/`.bat` on Windows, where Python cannot start them otherwise. A URL never reaches a command line, and `tests/fixes.py` checks it | fixed |
| 30 | build | ~2 300 lines in one module, no type hints, broad `except` | harder to review | maintainer decision: keep one file (a split costs disk reads for the coding agent and buys nothing at runtime) | by design |
| 31 | startup | `--caps` costs ~145 ms (browser discovery, `which`, JSON) | 5-15 % of a fast read | **maintainer decision: do not cache it** - `--caps` is what people run right after installing a language pack or `curl_cffi`, and a day-long cache would then lie. The cost is paid once per run | by design |
| 32 | time | no whole-run deadline; the worst chain on a wall with readers was ~105 s (20 HTTP + 10 converters + 20 browser + 20 readers + 20 archive + 4 pictures), 10 s more on a site whose API hangs | a hostile slow page occupied a call past every harness's timeout | fixed (row 60): one 55 s deadline for every read, each step's own bound inside it, what arrived kept; the table is in `references/engines.md` | fixed |
| 33 | cookies | the jar was a flat name -> value map per exact host: `Domain` ignored (a cookie for `example.com` never reached `app.example.com`), cookies set after a redirect were stored under the starting host, two runs on one host overwrote each other, pictures were fetched without cookies, a dead session looked like a wall | a site that needs a session still failed, and the failure was blamed on the site | 0.4.2: entries carry `Domain` and their own expiry, saving happens under the host that set the cookie, saves merge, both HTTP tiers share one jar, picture requests send it, and `--clear-cookies` (was `--fresh-session`) plus `signals.cookies` make a stale session visible | fixed |
| 34 | cookies | `Path` and `Secure` were ignored: a path-scoped cookie went to every path of the host, a `Secure` cookie would also travel over plain http | a cookie could be sent where the site did not mean to send it | fixed: an entry keeps `Path` and the `Secure` flag, and both are checked before a cookie is put on a request (the plain-`curl` tier gets a pre-filtered file, so its own jar reader cannot disagree) | fixed |
| 36 | platform | under WSL the Windows PATH is inherited, so `which defuddle` returned the npm shim under `/mnt/c`; starting a Windows process from Linux costs its full timeout | every document/page read paid 20 s; a 10-second property run took 7 minutes | fixed: `foreign_binary()` ignores `/mnt/...` and `.exe`/`.cmd`/`.bat` under WSL, for tools and for browsers | fixed |
| 37 | process control | a converter or a browser that spawned children kept the output pipe open, so `subprocess.run(timeout=)` could wait far past the timeout while draining | a hung child could hold a call open (measured 68 s on a trickling page) | fixed: the child runs in its own process group and the group is killed (`kill_tree`); the per-attempt bound now holds (68 s -> 44 s with three browsers, 24 s -> the configured bound in the test) | fixed |
| 38 | assets | pictures were re-downloaded for every URL of a site | wasted traffic on crawls | fixed, and measured honestly: the store is keyed by the **picture's** host (`assets.json` maps URL -> file, hard-linked), so a CDN is shared across every site that uses it. What it buys depends on the site: **Wikipedia 6 articles - 64 of 318 requests (20 %), 0.8 of 11.8 MB (7 %)**; **theguardian.com front page + 4 articles - 0 of 26 requests**, because every article image has a unique URL there. Repeated reads of the same page are covered by the page cache (0 requests) and by ETag revalidation (1 request, 0 bytes), not by this store | fixed |
| 39 | redirects | a `<meta http-equiv="refresh">` used to be invisible: the HTTP status was 200, so a stub page ("this moved here") came back as `empty` | one wasted read per moved page | fixed: one hop is followed when the page has almost no text, checked for a private target, recorded in `signals.meta_refresh` | fixed |
| 40 | naming | a downloaded file was always `raw.<ext guessed from the type>` | a link like `/download?id=7` lost the server's own name | fixed: `Content-Disposition` (`filename` and `filename*`) names the file, the type still decides the extension | fixed |
| 41 | politeness | `X-Robots-Tag: noai`/`noindex` is not read; nothing in the skill mentions it | a site that asks not to be used for AI training is read anyway | by design and said out loud in `README`/`SKILL.md`: this is the same product question as `robots.txt`, and the maintainer decided "a single user-requested read behaves like a browser" | by design |
| 42 | documents | `tesseract` reads only the language packs it has; the Windows installer ships `eng` + `osd` only, and a missing pack produced garbage or an empty page without a word about why | a Russian/German/Japanese scan stayed unread and looked like a broken skill | fixed: the installed packs are listed (`--caps` -> `ocr_langs`), a requested pack that is absent is reported (`signals.ocr_missing_langs`) and named in `improve[]` with the exact `*.traineddata` to install, and OCR falls back to whatever is installed instead of failing. `READ_PAGE_OCR_LANGS=rus+eng` for Russian; `READ_PAGE_TESSDATA` for a data folder outside the install | fixed |
| 43 | disk | the host asset store wrote a **second copy** of every picture the page already had (a 36.8 MB article folder came with a 36.7 MB store) | the cache cost roughly doubled after the store was introduced | fixed: the store hard-links the page's own file (`asset_store(..., src=dest)`, `os.link`), and a picture taken from the store is linked into the page folder - one inode, two names. Measured on a fresh cache: 10 pages x 6 shared pictures = 0.4 MB total, the store itself 0 bytes | fixed |
| 44 | cookies | the jar reached the HTTP tiers and the pictures, but the **headless browser and `yt-dlp` got no cookies at all**, and a cookie a page set in JavaScript was thrown away with the browser context | a members-only page that only renders for a logged-in session read as `empty`, and its session was lost between runs | fixed: `pw_cookies()` hands the jar to Playwright (Domain cookies as `domain`+`path`, host-only ones as `url`, port kept), `save_browser_cookies()` puts what the page set back into the same per-host jar, and `yt-dlp` gets a Netscape file via `--cookies`. Measured on a local gate that serves the article only with `Cookie: pass=1`: `status=empty, 7 chars, sent=0` before, `status=ok, 1596 chars, sent=1` in 4.3 s after. Remote readers are still never given a session | fixed |
| 45 | network | `curl` was handed the output path and the header path in argv; `curl.exe` receives argv in the **ANSI code page**, so on an English Windows any non-ASCII folder (`--dir C:\Users\Иван\...`, a Cyrillic `%LOCALAPPDATA%`) made it fail with status `000`, no bytes and **no error text** - the manifest said `error` with an empty reason | a unicode folder was unreadable on the plain-curl tier, which is the only tier a machine without `curl_cffi` has | fixed: curl runs with `cwd` set to an ASCII staging folder (`cache/tmp/<hex>`) and only relative names in argv (`body.part`, `body.hdr`, `cookies.txt`); Python moves the body into place and merges the jar back. A curl exit that produced no HTTP status is now recorded as `curl exit N: ...` instead of an empty status 0. Found by CI on windows-latest, where `--dir` used Cyrillic names | fixed |
| 46 | batch | one call reads one URL; there is no `--jsonl`/list mode, so a caller with ten links makes ten calls | a caller that cannot parallelise pays the per-process cost ten times | by design, measured: median 2.6 s per page of which ~0.3 s is start-up, so a *sequential* batch would cost ~26 s where ten parallel calls cost the slowest one (~3-5 s); a ten-page answer would also be up to 10 x 24 000 chars (~60-70k tokens), which the caller would have to truncate - losing exactly what it asked for; and a batch pays for pages the third answer already made unnecessary. Batch and crawl belong to crawlers and server-side APIs (MCP has no batch primitive: the host parallelises calls). The escape hatch is the caller's own loop. Revisit when three different callers ask for it, or transcripts show long chains of single reads for one question, or a host charges per call and cannot run calls in parallel | by design |
| 47 | pictures | `--screenshots N` returns as many tiles as the page needs up to `N`; nothing is scaled, nothing is capped, and a 30 000 px page with `--screenshots 20` costs ~55k tokens. One tile is ~2 700 tokens (1280x1568 at w*h/750) | by design |

## Decisions recorded (not bugs)

- **No `robots.txt`.** One user-requested read behaves like a browser; a bulk run would be a
  different tool. Revisit if a site complains.
- **Modules, one process a read.** `readpage.py` holds the read's flow; each newer mechanism lives in
  a module of its own in `scripts/` (the one-module rule was lifted on 2026-11-05).
- **No credit counter.** A key in the environment is consent to spend it; README says the price.
- **Files outlive text.** The page answer is cached for 15 minutes, the files stay until the daily
  prune or until the OS clears the temp folder.
- **Cookies are per site for 6 hours, sliding.** They are what makes a second read of the same site
  work; `--clear-cookies` is the escape hatch, and the advice names it when a wall may be ours.
- **No ad or tracker list in the browser (measured).** The browser renders few reads (4 of 71 in a
  measured run); on 67 pages that need it, 366 of 6 517 requests (5.6 %) went to names on a popular
  ad-block DNS filter, and on the machine measured a DNS-level ad blocker refused all but 10 of them
  already. Were it done, it would be that list downloaded and checked inside netguard (189 k
  names, daily, GPL-3.0 - fetched, not bundled), never a third-party DNS: the page's host names
  would leave the machine. The A/B, with nothing else blocking (2026-11-12, the same 67 pages, each
  read twice by a fresh process, the order alternating; `READ_PAGE_TEST_BLOCKLIST`): the list
  refused 341 names on 34 pages and cut responses by 5 % (6 407 -> 6 057), but the reads did not get
  faster - wall p50 7.48 -> 7.79 s, sum 554 -> 561 s; browser step p50 4.98 -> 5.06 s. One page
  gained much (ESPN 14.8 -> 7.8 s), the rest moved within noise; texts differed only where a page
  changes between reads anyway (feeds, shops), and vk.com/durov answered `blocked` without the list
  and `ok` with it, once. Not worth a 189 k-name list to fetch and keep current.
